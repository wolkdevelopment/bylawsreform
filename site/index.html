        How to read this page

        1. Start with the seven standards. They are simple tests any set of bylaws should pass.
        2. Then read the repairs. Each one says what is wrong today and what the repair does.
        3. To see the legal wording, open See the exact wording. Words being removed are crossed out, like this. New words are underlined, like this.
        4. Every repair starts out included. Switch one off to leave it out, and the scores at the top change right away.
        5. When you're ready, the page writes the amendments for the repairs you kept.
        6. Tell us what you think. Every repair has an Add a comment button.

        Each repair also shows its number in the formal submission, such as “Submitted as Amendment 13.” Those are the numbers used in emails and at meetings.

        To make the text bigger, use the A+ and A++ buttons at the top of the screen.

        What happens next

        The formal submission to the county chair turns these repairs into 42 bylaw amendments and 6 standing rule amendments. Read the formal submission, with the cover letter and the exact text of every amendment.

        1. Decide which repairs to keep. Switch off any you disagree with.
        2. Press Write the amendments. The page writes the exact wording, one amendment for each repair.
        3. Ask the Parliamentarian to check the wording.
        4. Submit the bylaw amendments in writing to the county chair at least 14 days before the meeting (Bylaw 10.1). They go to members with the meeting notice (Bylaw 10.2).
        5. At the meeting, any member can ask for a separate vote on any amendment. Bylaw amendments need a two-thirds vote. Standing rule amendments need a majority.
